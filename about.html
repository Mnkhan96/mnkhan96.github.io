Research profile

I am a PhD candidate in Chemistry at the Lahore University of Management Sciences (LUMS), working under the supervision of Dr. Rahman Shah Zaib Saleem ↗. My doctoral research combines experimental and computational investigations of donor–acceptor small molecules with potential applications in organic photovoltaics and related optoelectronics.

My research began with naphthyl chalcones and developed into scaffold-engineering projects involving pyrazoline–thiazole hybrids, thiophene-functionalized chalcones, and rigidified indane-1,3-dione systems. Collaborative work on 2-aminoimidazolones, quinoxaline carboxamides, and organoselenium compounds has broadened that perspective.

I combine reaction planning, synthesis, purification, and structural characterization with UV–visible and photoluminescence spectroscopy, cyclic voltammetry, and DFT/TD-DFT calculations. My central interest is establishing meaningful structure–property relationships: how donor strength, acceptor character, conjugation, and structural rigidity influence molecular response.

Alongside research, I have taught in undergraduate and graduate chemistry courses, mentored junior researchers, and contributed to departmental representation and academic service.

Methods & tools

Working across experiment and theory.

Synthetic organic chemistry

Multistep synthesis; Claisen–Schmidt and Knoevenagel condensations; Suzuki–Miyaura coupling; heterocyclic ring formation; reaction optimization; chromatographic purification and spectroscopic interpretation.

Computational chemistry

DFT and TD-DFT; geometry optimization; frequency analysis; frontier orbitals; density of states; electrostatic potentials; transition-density analysis; reorganization energies and transfer integrals.
