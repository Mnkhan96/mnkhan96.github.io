Pyrazoline–thiazole hybrids for organic optoelectronics: Exploring structure–property relationships via synthesis, photophysical, electrochemistry, and DFT study

Muhammad Naeem Khan, Syed Muhammad Umer, Kainat Ahmed, et al.

Journal of Photochemistry and Photobiology A: Chemistry, 481, 117426.

Turning a chalcone framework into pyrazoline–thiazole hybrids creates a new platform for tuning excited-state behavior.

The research question

How does transforming a chalcone into a pyrazoline–thiazole hybrid change its optical response, and which functional groups give the most useful balance of properties?

How we approached it

We used sequential heterocyclic synthesis to develop a pyrazoline–thiazole series and varied the functional groups around the new scaffold. Solvent-dependent absorption and fluorescence measurements were combined with cyclic voltammetry and DFT/TD-DFT calculations. This connected the synthetic changes to redox behavior and the nature of the excited states.

What we found

Strong positive solvatochromism and Stokes shifts exceeding 150 nm indicated substantial intramolecular charge transfer. Ester- and amide-functionalized derivatives were particularly emissive. The N-isopropyl carboxamide derivative gave the most favorable balance of fluorescence efficiency, exciton binding energy, and calculated charge-transport characteristics within the series.

Why it matters

Scaffold transformation can open design possibilities that substituent changes on the starting framework alone may not offer. Here, amide functionalization emerged as a useful handle for adjusting charge transfer and emission in the hybrid molecules.

My contribution

I independently conceived and led this extension of my chalcone research. I planned and performed the synthesis and experimental studies, analyzed photophysical and electrochemical data, carried out DFT/TD-DFT calculations, and prepared the manuscript. Syed Muhammad Umer provided synthetic assistance, collaborators supported NMR and HRMS characterization, and my supervisor provided scientific guidance.

The design lesson

Changing the scaffold and its functional groups creates complementary ways to control photophysical behavior.

Scope of the work

Calculated transport descriptors and molecular measurements guide candidate selection; photovoltaic device performance was not demonstrated.
