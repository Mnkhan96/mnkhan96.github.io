Tunable 2-aminoimidazolone donor–acceptor chromophores: Structure–property relationships from photophysical, electrochemical, and DFT studies

Muhammad Naeem Khan, Humera Baig, Aansa Hanif, et al.

Journal of Photochemistry and Photobiology A: Chemistry, 481, 117447.

How changing the donor–acceptor arrangement in 2-aminoimidazolones shifts the balance between charge redistribution and fluorescence.

The research question

Can a compact heterocyclic scaffold be tuned to favor either stronger intramolecular charge transfer or brighter emission?

How we approached it

We prepared nine 2-aminoimidazolone derivatives using one-pot and amine-diversification strategies. The series compared A–π–D and D–π–D arrangements, allowing electronic architecture and substitution to be examined together. Absorption and photoluminescence measurements, electrochemistry, and DFT/TD-DFT calculations provided complementary views of the ground and excited states.

What we found

The acceptor-containing A–π–D molecules showed stronger charge-transfer character and narrower energy gaps. The D–π–D analogues generally retained more localized excited states and stronger fluorescence. Within the series, the nitro-substituted derivative produced the most pronounced charge-transfer response and the largest Stokes shift.

Why it matters

Changing a molecular framework affects more than the position of an absorption peak. It can change where an excitation is localized and how a molecule releases its energy. The comparison provides a practical design lesson: a substitution pattern that strengthens charge transfer does not necessarily maximize emission.

My contribution

This project began collaboratively with Dr. Humera Baig. As she approached graduation, I took primary responsibility for continuing the synthesis, completing the remaining experimental work, analyzing the data, and carrying out DFT/TD-DFT calculations. I integrated the photophysical and electrochemical findings and prepared the manuscript, with scientific oversight from my supervisor and specialized characterization support from collaborators.

The design lesson

Molecular architecture provides a way to tune the competition between charge transfer and light emission.

Scope of the work

The study evaluates molecular properties and candidates for further optoelectronic investigation; it does not report a fabricated photovoltaic device.
