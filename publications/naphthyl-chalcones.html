Synthesis, photophysical, electrochemical, and DFT studies of naphthyl chalcones for photovoltaic applications

Muhammad Naeem Khan, Kainat Ahmed, Leena Siddiqui, et al.

Journal of Molecular Structure, 1340, 142526.

An integrated experimental and computational study of how substituent and naphthyl positional changes influence conjugated chalcones.

The research question

How do donor substitution and the position of a naphthyl group influence the optical and electrochemical properties of a synthetically accessible chalcone?

How we approached it

I selected a naphthyl-chalcone series and prepared the compounds through Claisen–Schmidt condensation. We then examined absorption, fluorescence, and electrochemical behavior alongside DFT/TD-DFT calculations. The common synthetic platform made it possible to compare changes in substitution and naphthyl connectivity.

What we found

The compounds were obtained in 60–85% yields and absorbed mainly at 380–420 nm, with visible fluorescence and pronounced Stokes shifts. Stronger electron-donating groups promoted charge transfer and excited-state reorganization. The 4-(dimethylamino)-substituted 1-naphthyl and 2-naphthyl derivatives offered the most favorable combination of fluorescence, charge-transfer character, and electrochemical properties within the study.

Why it matters

This work established the foundation for my later scaffold-engineering projects. It showed how a relatively accessible condensation chemistry could support a detailed comparison of molecular structure and optoelectronic response, leading naturally to questions about bridge modification and heterocyclic transformation.

My contribution

I conceived and independently led this study, designed and performed the syntheses, carried out the photophysical and electrochemical investigations, completed the DFT/TD-DFT analyses, interpreted the trends, and drafted the manuscript. My supervisor guided the project, while collaborators supported specialized NMR and HRMS characterization.

The design lesson

Simple synthetic changes can expose clear relationships between molecular structure and excited-state response.

Scope of the work

The work identifies molecules for further photovoltaic evaluation; it does not report fabricated solar cells.
