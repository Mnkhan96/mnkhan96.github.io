March 22–26, 2026 · ACS Spring 2026 · Atlanta, USA

Learning to tell a complete research story

From the synthesis bench at LUMS to the ACS Spring meeting in Atlanta, my naphthyl chalcone project travelled with a question: how do deliberate molecular changes influence optical and electronic properties? At the poster, I connected synthesis and characterization with photophysical, electrochemical and DFT analyses. Presenting them together helped me explain not just what we measured, but why.

The most useful part was scientific exchange. Questions about structure–property relationships, limitations and what should be tested next pushed me to distinguish calculated promise from demonstrated device performance. A conference is valuable not because of its size alone, but because it helps turn a finished project into better research questions.

Alhamdulillah, I am grateful to LUMS and SBASSE for sponsoring my visit and to Dr. Rahman Shah Zaib Saleem for his supervision and encouragement.

November 3–5, 2025 · 1st RSC–E-MRS–ACS Qatar Joint Conference

Meeting the wider materials-science community

In Doha, I presented “Synthesis, Photophysical, Electrochemical, and DFT Studies of Naphthyl Chalcones for Photovoltaic Applications.” The meeting brought chemists and materials researchers into the same conversation about a sustainable future. Explaining my work to that broader audience made me think carefully about how chemical structure, measurable properties and practical application fit together.

I found the contrast in perspectives especially valuable: synthesis tells us how to build a molecule, spectroscopy and electrochemistry tell us what it does, and conversations with materials scientists remind us to ask about stability and function. I am thankful to the conference organizers and its president, Prof. Mohammed Al-Hashimi, FRSC, for the opportunity and my certificate of presentation.

November 12, 2025 · 4th International Conference of Sciences · RWU

Explaining the science behind a molecular change

At Rawalpindi Women University, I delivered an oral presentation on naphthyl chalcone-based organic semiconductors. I aimed to tell a clear scientific story: from Claisen–Schmidt synthesis through absorption and emission spectra, electrochemical behavior and DFT calculations, to how substituent engineering changes the electronic response.

The important lesson was that a strong oral presentation does not need to claim a future solar-cell efficiency. It should explain what the measurements support, what remains a hypothesis and why the next experiment matters. Receiving the certificate was encouraging. I thank the organizers, including Prof. Dr. Bushra Mirza, Prof. Dr. M. Zaman Ashraf and Dr. Tunzeel Iqbal.

November 19–20, 2025 · 7th International Conference on Recent Trends in Chemistry · AIOU

Research reaches further when it can be explained

The theme “Chemistry for a Sustainable Future: From Research to Practical Impact” brought me back to a question every researcher should ask: how does a carefully designed experiment connect to a larger need? At AIOU, scientific discussions gave me a chance to practice answering that question beyond my immediate research group.

Whether explaining molecular design, discussing spectra or responding to a question, I was reminded that communicating science is itself part of doing good science. It helps us identify assumptions, improve explanations and find people interested in related problems.

2026 · RSC Sustainable Laboratories Grants · LUMS

Can better chemistry make a more sustainable laboratory?

Research is not just about the molecules we make. It is also about the solvent we use, the consumables we discard and the safety of the practices we introduce. I am a collaborator on CIRCLe—Circular, Integrated Reconditioning & Recovery for Consumables & Lab Liquids—at LUMS.

The project aims to safely recondition pipette tips and autosampler vials and recover solvents through micro-distillation. The Royal Society of Chemistry lists Shahzad Ameen and Irfan Ullah as the grant leads. I am grateful to contribute as a collaborator to the wider team, not as principal investigator.

The broader lesson is that sustainable laboratory work must protect the quality and reproducibility of experiments as well as reduce waste. Selection for the RSC's Sustainable Laboratories Grants programme gives the team an opportunity to explore that challenge.
