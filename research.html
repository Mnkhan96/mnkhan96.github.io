Research

Designing molecules.
Understanding their response.

My research follows a common question across different molecular platforms: how does changing a structure change its interaction with light and charge?

01 / Chalcone platforms

Accessible synthesis.
Systematic comparison.

Chalcones provide a versatile starting point for comparing donor substitution, aromatic connectivity, and conjugation. My naphthyl-chalcone work established a platform for relating synthetic changes to absorption, fluorescence, and redox behavior.

Subsequent work explores thiophene-containing bridges and donor–acceptor engineering to extend these structure–property comparisons.

Read the naphthyl-chalcone story →
02 / Scaffold engineering

Changing the molecular framework.

Converting a chalcone framework into pyrazoline–thiazole hybrids introduces new functionalization possibilities. Rigidified indanedione-based systems offer another way to examine how molecular motion and conjugation affect excited-state behavior.

The aim is to compare structural changes through matched experimental and computational measurements.

Read the hybrid-chromophore story →
03 / Heterocyclic chromophores

Donor–acceptor architecture.

Studies of 2-aminoimidazolones and quinoxaline carboxamides examine how heterocyclic cores and peripheral substituents alter charge redistribution, fluorescence, and electronic structure.

Comparing different architectures helps separate the effects of the core scaffold from those of a donor or acceptor substituent.
